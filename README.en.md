# 西醫新法（[1872]；Bodleian Sinica 1826） / Xi yi xin fa [New methods of Western medicine]: Transcription dataset

v0.2.0 · Chu, Chen-Yu

Xi yi xin fa [New methods of Western medicine] is a late-Qing Chinese medical work concerning surgery and bandaging, credited to John Glasgow Kerr (嘉約翰) for oral translation and Lin Xiangdong (林湘東) for written rendering. The official fixed catalogue for Bodleian Libraries, Sinica 1826, supplies [Canton] and [1872]. Derived from the Bodleian digital images, this dataset provides an AI-assisted transcription of original printed text across six figure faces, thirty-seven main-text faces, and one face with an original printed frame and a blank body. TXT, JSON, and TEI XML retain readings, uncertainties, and physical-face-to-image mappings for research on late-Qing surgical and bandaging terminology in translation, image-text relationships, and print history. This AI-assisted revised edition follows comparison with second image observations and source review; uncertainty and unresolved cases remain. Full human double collation and expert review have not been performed.

44 physical faces; 472 stable units; 9,189 Unicode code points; 71 current uncertainty records; 15 source marginal regions with uncertain text or page assignment. Code-point and uncertainty counts are not character counts or error rates.

Files: `book.txt`, `book.json`, and `book.tei.xml`. The source object, IIIF manifest and fixed catalogue are linked in [PROVENANCE.md](PROVENANCE.md). Verify quotations against the Bodleian images and cite the fixed version. Revised-version DOI: [10.5281/zenodo.23971701](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23971701). Repository: https://github.com/poplolioi11/bodleian-sinica1826-xiyi-xinfa-1872.

The original unit identifiers and ordering are retained. This edition applies 215 recorded local operations; 215 comparison candidates remain unresolved. See [REVISION-CHANGES.json](REVISION-CHANGES.json), [METHODS.md](METHODS.md) and [QUALITY.md](QUALITY.md).

Historical text and faithful mechanical transcription acquire no new exclusive restriction. Copyrightable original documentation and arrangement are CC BY 4.0. Source images are excluded and retain their own conditions; see [RIGHTS.md](RIGHTS.md). Intended for historical-document scholarship, not contemporary clinical guidance.
